What data is available?

Our bridge game database is a rich source of information for games played in Albany and Corvallis since around Oct 2010 to the present. (That is when Unit 477 invested in the dealing machine which provided hand records of pre-dealt hands, as well as in Bridgemate table scoring devices which allowed recording of the contract, the declarer, the score, and later, the opening lead.

For every event we have recorded the time and date, the rating and type of game, the players, their assigned flight (strat), their score, both numerically and by percentage, their masterpoint award, including point color, and their final rank.

For games which used Bridgemates, all contract details are available, as well as the opening lead.

For games using pre-dealt hands, each hand is recorded, as well as the "Double Dummy" analysis of each hand with the calculated par contract(s).

What good is this data?

This data can show your performance with each of your partners. You can track your progress and skill level advancement over time by monitoring your score percentage. You can find how often you and your partner are declarers, and how often you are defenders. You can separate your defensive score from your 'offensive' (declarer) score, and compare and find where you can improve your game. You can compare how well you score with each of your regular partners. You could answer questions about how often you overbid, how often your sacrifice is successful, vs. how often your sacrifice was unnecessary. All these measures can be charted over time.

Mind you, we are not offering all of these analyses right now. Many of them would require extensive data analysis which we are prepared to develop if there is sufficient interest.

If you think of an analysis that you'd like to see, please send us your suggestion in the request box on the queries page. For example, we could answer questions such as these:

  • "Do I do better when our side gets more high cards? - or conversely, do I do worse when the opponents get more high cards?

  • "Do I do better when I am declarer, or when the opponents declare? Or the converse, do I score better when I defend?

  • "How often do I declare? How often do I defend? Is there a correlation between our success and how often I declare? (defend?)

  • "Am I bidding enough? That is, if I look at the makeable contracts, are my partner and I missing out by not bidding high enough?

  • "Am I bidding too much? Do I let the opponents push me to a level too high?

  • "Do I double our opponents often enough?

  • "Am I bidding sacrifices often enough? Am I taking too many false sacrifices?"

    • So many questions - so few answers! Are the answers in our future? Maybe. They are certainly available in our data - no question there! All we need to do is tease them out.

Limitations

  1. For team games, data is limited since neither pre-dealt hands nor Bridgemates are used - so, no contracts and no hands.

  2. During Howell movements, many pairs change direction as they move from table to table, and there is no way to identify which player sat E vs W, nor who sat N vs S. We can, however, generally tell whether you were defending or declaring.

  3. We have no access to ACBL tournament data, nor do we have results for online games.
